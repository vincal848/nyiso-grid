# Industry Practice in LMP Modeling and Forecasting (US ISOs, NYISO emphasis)

Research date: 2026-09-28. Evidence labels: [IND] = independent (ISO/market monitor/academic); [VENDOR] = vendor marketing/product claims; [SECONDARY] = aggregator/blog.

## 1. Production-cost / SCUC-SCED simulation models (PLEXOS, PROMOD, Dayzer, Aurora, MAPS, PowerSIMM, etc.)

### Takeaway
Commercial production-cost models forecast LMPs by replicating the ISO's security-constrained unit commitment and economic dispatch on a full network model, driven by fuel prices, load forecasts, unit and transmission outages and emissions costs. Their accuracy is almost never published independently. The public validation work that does exist (e.g., the Irish SEM PLEXOS backcasts) stresses that one-year backcasts cannot validate a model and that over-calibrating to history causes overfitting. For a public-data desk, the real value of these tools is structural "what-if" analysis of outages and constraints, not point accuracy.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR] DAYZER (Cambridge Energy Solutions) "simulates the operation of the electricity markets by mimicking the dispatch procedures used by the corresponding ISOs" and replicates the ISO's security-constrained least-cost commitment and dispatch in the DA market. It forecasts DA hourly zonal or nodal LMPs, shadow prices and congestion costs under what-if scenarios, using the latest fuel prices, demand forecasts, unit and transmission-line outages and emission permit costs. It covers NYISO, NEPOOL, PJM, MISO, ERCOT, WECC and Ontario. — [CES Dayzer product page](https://www.ces-us.com/product-dayzer.asp); [Dayzer brochure](http://www.ces-us.com/download/dayzer%20v3.5-101.pdf)
- [VENDOR/SECONDARY] PROMOD (Hitachi Energy) forecasts LMP for specific nodes, user-defined hubs, or load- or generator-weighted zones. It is described as having more than 45 years of history and as a "gold standard" production-cost model. This comes from a blog comparison, not an accuracy study. — [PROMOD vs PLEXOS blog](https://futureofcomputing.beehiiv.com/p/tool-use-production-cost-modeling-promod-versus-plexos)
- [IND] Regulator-published validation (Irish SEM Committee, PLEXOS): "It is difficult to categorically validate a model against historical market outcomes with a single year of data." Over-fitting to replicate one historical year makes a model "not appropriate to model future expected years." This is the clearest public regulatory backcast methodology I found, but it is European, not a US ISO. — [SEM PLEXOS Validation and Backcast 2019–25](https://www.semcommittee.com/files/semcommittee/media-files/SEM-20-004%20SEM%20PLEXOS%20Validation%20(2019-2025)%20and%20Backcast%20Report.pdf); [SEM PLEXOS 2021–2029 backcast](https://www.semcommittee.com/files/semcommittee/media-files/SEM-21-086%20SEM%20PLEXOS%20Model%20(2021-2029)%20Input%20Validation%20and%20Backcast%20Report.pdf)
- [VENDOR] Yes Energy warns that backcasts need "vintaged" (as-of) data. Without accurate point-in-time data, backcasts are inaccurate, and ML/backcast models should be constrained to the data available at the time of the event. — [Yes Energy: vintaging LMP data](https://www.yesenergy.com/blog/the-importance-of-vintaging-locational-marginal-pricing-data)
- [IND/SECONDARY] Job postings for FTR traders list power-flow and market-simulation tools such as PowerWorld, DAYZER and UPLAN. They also ask for research into supply, demand and congestion fundamentals, historical congestion patterns and transmission outages, plus SQL/Python/R skills. — [SimplyHired FTR trader listings](https://www.simplyhired.com/k-ftr-trader-jobs.html)
- [IND] An academic alternative to full SCUC simulation is Ji, Thomas and Tong's probabilistic RT LMP and congestion forecast. It is built on a DC-OPF with stochastic load and generation, and it produces LMP distributions and congestion probabilities (IEEE TPWRS / CERTS). — [CERTS: Probabilistic Forecasting of RT LMP and Network Congestion](https://certs.lbl.gov/publications/probabilistic-forecasting-real-time.html); [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel7/59/4374138/07514918.pdf)

### Inferences
- The inputs the vendors say they need are a network model with contingencies, unit heat rates and offer behavior, unit and transmission outages, fuel prices and load/renewable forecasts. A public-data desk can approximate each of these, but not at the fidelity of the ISO's own model.
- Typical horizons: Dayzer-type tools are sold for DA/next-days and weeks (DA replication). PROMOD, PLEXOS and Aurora are mostly used for months to years (planning, FTR annual auctions, forward curves). This is a common industry pattern, but none of the sources I fetched state it explicitly.
- Because vendors do not publish head-to-head accuracy numbers, a desk should expect to run its own vintaged backtests before relying on any simulation output.

### Gaps
- I found no published independent accuracy study (MAPE or pinball loss) comparing PROMOD, PLEXOS, Dayzer, Aurora, GE MAPS or PowerSIMM LMP forecasts against realized ISO LMPs, and no FERC or ISO comparison of these tools.
- I did not verify current ownership or product details for Aurora (Energy Exemplar), GE MAPS, Ascend PowerSIMM, Enverus, Genscape/Wood Mackenzie or Velocity Suite within this search budget. Treat any ownership claims about them as unverified.

## 2. Fundamental / heat-rate / supply-stack approaches

### Takeaway
The standard reduced-form fundamental model is a bid-stack (merit-order) curve: price is set where an aggregate inverse supply curve (fuel price × heat rate, by marginal fuel) meets largely inelastic demand. Implied heat rate (power price ÷ gas price) is modeled as a function of load or net load. The public inputs are EIA-860/923, EPA CEMS and ISO masked offers. In NYISO the masked offers are published with a 3-month lag and trackable masked IDs.

### Cited Findings
- [IND] Structural bid-stack models define electricity price as the intersection of an aggregate inverse supply curve with inelastic demand. In multi-fuel versions any fuel can become marginal, and closed-form derivative prices follow. — [Carmona, Coulon & Schwarz, multi-fuel structural approach (arXiv 1205.2299)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1205.2299)
- [IND] Form EIA-923 provides plant-level generation, fuel consumption and fuel cost data. — [EIA-923](https://www.eia.gov/electricity/data/eia923/)
- [IND/SECONDARY] Heat rates can be computed from CEMS (stack-measured heat input) or from EIA-923 fuel data, and the two sources can disagree. — [Andover Technology: CEMS vs fuel data for heat rate](https://www.andovertechnology.com/comparison-of-cems-and-fuel-data-for-heat-rate/)
- [IND] NYISO releases masked bid data (generator, ancillary service, load and transaction bids) with bidder identities hidden. A trackable masked ID lets individual bidders be followed over time. The lag was cut from 6 months to 3 months starting with the February 2010 posting. — [NYISO 3 Month Bid Data Release Description](http://mis.nyiso.com/public/postings/NYISO%203%20Month%20Bid%20Data%20Release%20Description_V1.pdf); [prior 6-month description](http://mis.nyiso.com/public/postings/NYISO%206%20Month%20Bid%20Data%20Release%20Description.pdf)
- [IND] An academic study of NYISO DA generator bidding behavior uses the masked bid data. — [Energy Economics, "Generators' bidding behavior in the NYISO day-ahead wholesale electricity market"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S014098830900098X)

### Inferences
- A practical recipe for a small desk: build a cost-based stack from EIA-860 capacity, CEMS/923 heat rates and daily gas prices by hub (Transco Z6 NY, Iroquois, Tennessee Z6, Dominion/TETCO for west NY). Then calibrate the markup, or the empirical offer curve shape, to the 3-month-lagged masked offers, matching units by masked ID. The 3-month lag makes masked offers good for structural calibration but useless for next-day bidding.
- Regional gas spreads matter for NY congestion, not just the price level (see section 5). A single-gas-price heat-rate model will miss Central-East and NYC congestion.

### Gaps
- I found no public practitioner paper showing error metrics for a supply-stack LMP model in NYISO specifically.
- I did not locate a public method document on de-masking or matching NYISO masked offers to specific units. Practitioners are believed to match by capacity and location, but I could not source this.

## 3. Congestion and shadow-price forecasting in practice

### Takeaway
Vendors sell congestion analytics as data plus workflow rather than as forecasts. That means historical DA/RT shadow prices, shift factors, binding-constraint histories, FTR/TCC auction constraint data, transmission outage feeds and new-build/retirement trackers. Constraint-level forecasting in practice combines outage schedules with shift factors and historical binding patterns. In NYISO, congestion is dominated by a handful of corridors and is highly sensitive to gas spreads and long outages.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR] Yes Energy provides historical DA and RT congestion, shift factors and shadow prices for analyzing congestion patterns and drivers. It also sells FTR auction constraint data covering multiple auctions. — [Yes Energy: FTR auction constraint data](https://www.yesenergy.com/blog/elevate-your-trading-with-ftr-auction-constraint-data); [Yes Energy FTR solutions](https://www.yesenergy.com/ftr-trading-solutions)
- [VENDOR] Yes Energy's data covers nodal prices, generation and transmission outages, transmission constraints, load, supply stack, weather and gas prices. Its "analysis-ready" products include bid-close/forecast stitching and net load forecasts (PowerSignals / DataSignals). — [Yes Energy PowerSignals](https://www.yesenergy.com/power-signals); [Yes Energy traders page](https://www.yesenergy.com/solutions/traders)
- [VENDOR] Yes Energy's Infrastructure Insights tracks planned generation, transmission and load facilities entering or retiring. It is marketed to FTR traders who need to anticipate structural grid changes that affect long-dated congestion. — [Infrastructure Insights](https://www.yesenergy.com/products/infrastructure-insights); [blog](https://www.yesenergy.com/blog/leveraging-new-build-dataset-in-ftr-trading)
- [VENDOR] Dayzer outputs shadow prices and congestion costs directly from its SCUC replication, so it forecasts congestion at the constraint level rather than statistically. — [CES Dayzer](https://www.ces-us.com/product-dayzer.asp)
- [IND] NYISO 2024: DA congestion revenue was $306M, down from $311M in 2023 and just above the decade low of $297M in 2020. Central-East's share fell from 53% (2023) to 24% (2024) after the AC Transmission Segment A/B projects were completed at the end of 2023 and Quebec net imports fell. The most congested corridors in 2024 were Central-East (24%), Long Island (19%), NYC 345 kV and load-pocket lines (17%), external interfaces (16%) and West-to-Central (15%, mainly Scriba–Volney 345 kV limiting Oswego output). NYC congestion rose from $15.7M to $52M, driven by transmission outages, with more than 40% occurring in two cold spells. On Long Island, one of two 345 kV upstate ties was out for about 200 days in each of 2023 and 2024. — [Potomac Economics, NYISO 2024 SOM (May 2025)](https://www.potomaceconomics.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/NYISO-2024-SOM-Full-Report_5-14-2025-final.pdf)
- [IND] NYISO 2025: DA congestion revenue was $638M, a 108% increase over 2024. The main driver was higher gas prices and wider inter-regional gas spreads, and nearly 70% of 2025 congestion occurred in January, February and December. Central-to-East NY paths were 23% of DA congestion revenue, with more than 80% of that in winter. Gas spreads also drove PJM→NY and NY→ISO-NE congestion. NYC congestion rose alongside extended transmission outages and tighter supply. — [Potomac Economics, NYISO 2025 SOM (May 2026)](https://www.potomaceconomics.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/NYISO-2025-SOM-Report__5-19-2026-final.pdf)
- [IND] Transmission outages are the primary driver of NYISO DA congestion shortfalls (TCC auction capability exceeding DA capability). 2024 had a net surplus of $3M versus a $59M shortfall in 2023. Balancing (RT) congestion shortfalls concentrate on about a dozen days, and external interfaces (especially primary PJM) made up more than $8M in 2024. — [NYISO 2024 SOM](https://www.potomaceconomics.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/NYISO-2024-SOM-Full-Report_5-14-2025-final.pdf)
- [IND] The North zone sees volatile north-to-central congestion from intermittent renewables, large and inflexible Quebec imports, and loop flows. — [NYISO 2024 SOM](https://www.potomaceconomics.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/NYISO-2024-SOM-Full-Report_5-14-2025-final.pdf)

### Inferences
- For NYISO, a constraint-level congestion model should use these features: (a) the inter-regional gas spread (e.g., Transco Z6 NY vs. Dominion South/Tennessee Z4 for Central-East and PJM/NE paths), (b) the transmission outage schedule for a short list of facilities (Long Island 345 kV ties, NYC 345 kV, Scriba–Volney, Central-East), (c) Quebec import levels, and (d) load and temperature, especially winter cold snaps. The swing from $306M to $638M in a single year, driven by gas spreads, shows that regime identification matters more than hourly model fit.
- Outage-schedule data is the highest-value public input for congestion. The SOM shows that the largest unanticipated TCC gains and losses came from long outages and structural transmission changes.

### Gaps
- I found no public accuracy figures for vendor congestion or shadow-price forecasts.
- I did not verify Enverus, ESAI or Genscape/Wood Mackenzie congestion product specifics within budget. ESAI publishes LMP/congestion commentary ([ESAI Power LMP tag](https://www.esaipower.com/tag/lmp/)), but I did not review product detail.

## 4. Quant/trading-firm approaches to DART (virtual bidding) and FTR/TCC; academic evidence

### Takeaway
Academic work shows that virtual bidding improves convergence and efficiency (CAISO) and that residual profits remain after transaction costs. However, arbitrage is limited by capital and transaction costs (MISO), and virtuals can profit from ISO modeling simplifications without improving dispatch. The FTR literature finds FTRs are, on average, sold below their realized value. PJM figures of about $300M/year in financial-participant profits are cited, and a PJM study attributes the gap to a trading premium rather than to auction design. Firms' public signals (job ads) point to fundamentals plus power-flow simulation plus statistical/historical congestion analysis.

### Cited Findings
- [IND] Jha & Wolak (CAISO): the introduction of convergence bidding reduced total variable costs, unit start-ups and energy used, all pointing to higher efficiency. They test market efficiency using trading-strategy profitability net of transaction costs. — [Jha & Wolak draft, Stanford](https://web.stanford.edu/group/fwolak/cgi-bin/sites/default/files/files/CAISO_VB_draft_V8.pdf)
- [IND] Li, Svoboda & Oren (J. Regulatory Economics 48:245–284, 2015): backtested trading strategies stayed consistently profitable after CAISO convergence bidding began on 1 Feb 2011, even net of transaction costs, but profitability fell significantly relative to before, which they read as an efficiency gain. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11149-015-9281-3); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/regeco/v48y2015i3p245-284.html)
- [IND] Parsons, Colbert, Larrieu, Martin & Mastrangelo (MIT CEEPR WP 15-002, 2015): the case for virtual bidding rests on an oversimplified model of the multi-settlement design. The compromises ISO algorithms make let virtual bidders profit without improving system performance, and this can add real costs. The paper includes a California case study. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2574397); [MIT PDF](https://www.mit.edu/~jparsons/publications/20150300_Financial_Arbitrage_and_Efficient_Dispatch.pdf)
- [IND] Birge, Hortaçsu, Mercadal & Pavlin (Energy Economics, 2018; MISO): financial traders had only a weak effect on the DA-RT premium. Two things limited them: restricted capital (shown by trading falling during the financial crisis) and regulatory transaction costs. Some financial players bet in the opposite direction of the pricing gap and sustained large losses. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0140988318303529); [CEEPR WP](https://ceepr.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/2017-003.pdf)
- [IND] Hadsell & Shawky (Energy Journal, 2006; NYISO peak hours 2001–2004): GARCH models show RT volatility is higher but less persistent than DA volatility. Congestion is an important driver of volatility, and the DA premium is related to RT volatility. Their later work found significant premiums and discounts in NYISO. — [Energy Journal](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.5547/ISSN0195-6574-EJ-Vol27-No2-9)
- [IND] FTR/TCC efficiency literature: Adamson et al. (2010) argue NYISO FTR/TCC market efficiency improved over time. Hadsell & Shawky (2009) suggest more competition could improve TCC auction efficiency. Opgrand, Preckel, Gotham & Liu (Energy Journal 43(3), 2022; PJM) find the gap between auction price and realized value reflects a trading premium, not auction flaws, and that ARR management strategies explain part of it. Other sources state FTRs sell on average below the revenue they generate, with about $300M/yr in PJM profits earned by financial participants. I saw these claims only as search snippets and did not read the papers in full. — [Opgrand et al. 2022](https://dx.doi.org/10.5547/01956574.43.3.jopg); [Energy Economics 2023, FTR vs. futures pricing inconsistency](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0140988323004437); [PJM FTR Market Review whitepaper 2020](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/library/reports-notices/special-reports/2020/ftr-market-review-whitepaper.pdf)
- [IND] O'Keefe examines unilateral market power in FTR auctions. — [ftr3.pdf](https://mfokeefe.github.io/research/papers/ftr3.pdf)
- [IND] ML/algorithmic virtual bidding papers (CAISO data) exist, including a portfolio-optimization approach on nodal DART spreads and ML-driven strategies. — [Algorithmic Bidding for Virtual Trading (arXiv 1802.03010)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.03010); [ML-Driven Virtual Bidding (arXiv 2104.02754)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.02754); [Virtual Trading in Multi-Settlement Markets (arXiv 2508.11979, 2025)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.11979)
- [SECONDARY] FTR trader job descriptions ask candidates to trade long-term, annual and monthly auctions and the secondary market, and to model supply, demand and congestion fundamentals. Strategies are to be built on historical congestion, transmission outages and market conditions, using tools such as PowerWorld, DAYZER and UPLAN. — [SimplyHired FTR trader](https://www.simplyhired.com/k-ftr-trader-jobs.html)

### Inferences
- The typical industry stack (from job ads and vendor offerings) is: a simulation engine (Dayzer/UPLAN/PROMOD) for expected congestion under outage scenarios, plus a data vendor for vintaged constraint and outage history, plus statistical or historical-analog models. The public signals support this, but no firm publicly documents its models.
- Parsons et al. and Birge et al. imply that persistent DART edges often come from structural ISO modeling differences (e.g., DA vs. RT network or reserve modeling) and capital or risk limits, not from forecast skill alone. That suits a small desk that studies ISO market rules closely.

### Gaps
- I found no conference talks or public model descriptions from DC Energy, Vitol, Citadel, Tower or Susquehanna power desks within this budget. The job-posting evidence came from aggregators, not the firms' own pages.
- I did not independently verify the "~$300M/yr PJM FTR profits" figure. It came from a search snippet, so trace it to the primary PJM IMM source before citing.

## 5. NYISO-specific: virtuals, TCCs, convergence (Potomac Economics SOM 2024 and 2025)

### Takeaway
Confirmed: NYISO virtual trading is allowed only at the load-zone level (11 zones), plus "virtual-like" imports and exports at external proxy buses. Virtual profitability is small in calm years ($0.06/MWh in 2024) and larger in volatile years ($1.02/MWh and $30.3M net in 2025). TCC returns swing sharply with unanticipated gas-spread and outage regimes: -66%, then -47%, then +135% over three consecutive 12-month periods. Long Island and NYC show structural RT premiums.

### Cited Findings
- [IND] "Virtual trading is currently allowed only at the load zone level." NYISO allows virtual traders "to arbitrage the price differences at the load zone level between day-ahead and real-time." Scheduled external transactions at proxy buses that do not flow in RT are treated as virtual imports and exports. — [NYISO 2024 SOM, Appendix](https://www.potomaceconomics.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/NYISO-2024-SOM-Full-Report_5-14-2025-final.pdf); [NYISO 2025 SOM, Appendix](https://www.potomaceconomics.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/NYISO-2025-SOM-Report__5-19-2026-final.pdf)
- [IND] 2024 virtuals: net profit about $1.8M (2023: about $1.2M). Internal zones earned +$3M and interfaces lost $1.2M, for an average of $0.06/MWh. By type, virtual supply cleared about 1,275 MW at +$0.82/MWh, virtual load about 1,089 MW at -$0.64/MWh, and virtual exports about 350 MW at -$1.27/MWh. The monitor's view: "low virtual profitability indicates that the markets are relatively well-arbitraged." — [NYISO 2024 SOM, Fig. 17](https://www.potomaceconomics.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/NYISO-2024-SOM-Full-Report_5-14-2025-final.pdf)
- [IND] 2025 virtuals: net profit about $30.3M. Internal zones earned +$45.8M and interfaces lost $15.5M, for an average of +$1.02/MWh. The report attributes this to higher energy prices and volatility. The most consistent losses were virtual exports at the primary New England interface, partly tied to REC sales to New England. — [NYISO 2025 SOM](https://www.potomaceconomics.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/NYISO-2025-SOM-Report__5-19-2026-final.pdf)
- [IND] The monitor screens for outsized virtual profits: about 1–8% of virtual MW per month earned or lost more than 50% of the zone price in 2023–24. "Large profits may be an indicator of a modeling inconsistency." — [NYISO 2024 SOM, Fig. A-36](https://www.potomaceconomics.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/NYISO-2024-SOM-Full-Report_5-14-2025-final.pdf)
- [IND] The monitor raised concerns about virtual imports and exports: large, consistent net-import-direction volumes, and treatment as available despite failing post-DAM checkout. It recommends NYISO find ways to identify virtual imports. — [NYISO 2024 SOM, Section on Virtual Imports/Exports](https://www.potomaceconomics.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/NYISO-2024-SOM-Full-Report_5-14-2025-final.pdf)
- [IND] Convergence in 2024: DA prices averaged above RT in all regions except NYC. The largest DA premiums came in January (gas volatility) and RT was higher in July (peak load and reserve shortages). — [NYISO 2024 SOM](https://www.potomaceconomics.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/NYISO-2024-SOM-Full-Report_5-14-2025-final.pdf)
- [IND] Convergence in 2025: West through Capital zones had a DA premium of 0.4%–2.3%, while Central had a 0.7% RT premium. All Southeast NY zones had RT above DA, with NYC at +4.7% and Long Island at +5.6%, driven by RT reserve shortages in heat waves and RT congestion. DA premiums peaked in January and December. Long Island is structurally prone to RT premiums because of slow-ramping older steam units, a less flexible local gas LDC, oil reliance and weak HV ties. — [NYISO 2025 SOM](https://www.potomaceconomics.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/NYISO-2025-SOM-Report__5-19-2026-final.pdf)
- [IND] TCC performance, Nov 2023–Oct 2024: net loss of $118M, a -47% return (prior 12 months: -66%). Inter-zonal paths averaged -66% and intra-zonal +8%. Losses of $117M on Central-East, into-SENY and NY→NE paths coincided with a 54% fall in DA congestion on those paths that was "not well anticipated at the time of the auctions." — [NYISO 2024 SOM, Table 9](https://www.potomaceconomics.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/NYISO-2024-SOM-Full-Report_5-14-2025-final.pdf)
- [IND] TCC performance, Nov 2024–Oct 2025: net profit of $234M, a +135% return (inter-zonal +117%, intra-zonal +152%). This included about $89M of gains on PJM↔NY and NY↔NE paths from widening gas spreads, and about $97M within NYC from a 195% rise in NYC-path DA congestion (gas prices plus extended outages). — [NYISO 2025 SOM, Table 8](https://www.potomaceconomics.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/NYISO-2025-SOM-Report__5-19-2026-final.pdf)
- [IND] The monitor's recurring conclusion: TCC prices "generally align with congestion levels anticipated at the time of the auctions." P&L correlates with year-over-year changes in DA congestion patterns, unexpected long unplanned outages are a key driver, and market expectations of congestion improve closer to real time. — [NYISO 2024 SOM](https://www.potomaceconomics.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/NYISO-2024-SOM-Full-Report_5-14-2025-final.pdf); [NYISO 2025 SOM](https://www.potomaceconomics.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/NYISO-2025-SOM-Report__5-19-2026-final.pdf)
- [IND] DA congestion residuals arise when DA network capability differs from what was modeled in the TCC auctions. The monitor recommends allocating residuals by changes in scheduled transmission utilization rather than in proportion to TCC revenues. — [NYISO 2024 SOM](https://www.potomaceconomics.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/NYISO-2024-SOM-Full-Report_5-14-2025-final.pdf)

### Inferences
- NYISO DART products are zonal, so the desk's nodal DA/RT decomposition matters mainly for TCC valuation and for understanding zonal price formation, not for placing virtuals. Zonal LBMPs are load-weighted aggregates of nodal prices, so nodal congestion forecasts still feed zonal DART signals.
- The NYC and Long Island RT premiums (reserve shortages, slow ramping, gas LDC inflexibility) point to asymmetric, fat-tailed RT distributions. This supports probabilistic (quantile) forecasting over point forecasts for DART in zones J and K.
- The TCC P&L swings (-47% to +135% in consecutive years) show that the prices of the 6-month and 1-year capability-period auctions embed last year's congestion regime. A desk with a good regime view (gas spreads, outage schedules, transmission additions such as the upcoming CHPE and further AC transmission) may find edge. Positions are lumpy and risky, though.

### Gaps
- Some rows of the zone-by-zone TCC profit tables (2024 Table 9, 2025 Table 8) were garbled by PDF text extraction, so I cited only numbers stated in the report text. Verify the row-level figures in the original PDFs.
- I did not retrieve NYISO TCC auction mechanics (Centralized TCC Auction rounds, reconfiguration auction timing) from primary NYISO manuals within budget. Consult the NYISO TCC Manual separately.
